Strategy Memo

Clear Path — Phased Vision

A cleaner, more readable strategy brief for launching Clear Path in Raleigh first, proving real value early, then expanding into treasury, controlled spend, and the original mission-driven support infrastructure.

Updated: 2026-03-23

Executive Summary

Core idea: launch Clear Path as a high-trust financial operating layer for churches and nonprofits first, then expand into treasury, controlled spend, and ultimately the original support mission.

Phase 1

Save money. Build trust. Prove ROI in Raleigh.

Phase 2

Turn trust into treasury adoption and aligned yield participation.

Phase 3

Turn treasury into controlled spend and real card-program economics.

Phase 4

Realize the original mission with proven infrastructure behind it.

Guiding Principles

Launch local first

Raleigh, Cary, and the Triangle are the right starting point. Local trust is part of the product.

Lead with obvious value

Fee savings, ACH migration, restricted-fund clarity, and better reporting are easier to sell than abstract future vision.

Stay in neo-bank world

Clear Path should be the software orchestrator. Regulated partners should handle money movement, custody, yield, and card issuance.

Wallet-backed, not wallet-first

Users should experience governed business accounts with approvals and recovery—not raw self-custody wallet pain.

Phase 1 — Trust + Savings Wedge

Raleigh launch phase

Launch
“We helped you save ~2% and we’re just getting started.”
GoalWin 3–10 local organizations by proving that Clear Path can save money, improve donor trust, and make restricted funds easier to manage.
Target CustomersMid-sized churches, smaller nonprofits, and organizations already frustrated with Realm, Vanco, or legacy giving stacks.
Switch IncentiveACH migration with simple bank-linking and a one-time setup reward, fund credit, or campaign-level push for recurring donors.
Revenue Target$25K–$75Kannualized run-rate by end of phase

Core Value Proposition

  • Help organizations keep more of every gift
  • Reduce card-fee drag through ACH migration
  • Make restricted funds easier to trace and trust
  • Avoid ugly upfront software tax

What Clear Path Builds

  • Donor-facing giving flow
  • Admin dashboard
  • Internal ledger / source of truth
  • Fund designation logic
  • Restricted-fund reporting
  • ACH migration UX
  • Standard + premium reporting

What Partners Provide

  • ACH/card payment rails
  • Bank-linking / account verification
  • Regulated fund movement
  • Custody / account housing where needed

Raleigh launch target

  • 3–10 local organizations
  • 1 strong church case study
  • 1 nonprofit case study

  • Measurable designated-fund transparency results

Phase 2 — Treasury Layer + Yield Participation

Turn trust into treasury adoption

Treasury
“You already saved on giving. Now your designated balances can work harder while staying easier to manage and report on.”
GoalMove from cheaper, more transparent giving into better balance management and governed treasury accounts.
Target CustomersOrganizations already using Clear Path flows and willing to keep meaningful designated balances in-system.
Revenue ModelYield share becomes a meaningful aligned upside, supported by premium treasury/reporting features.
Revenue Target$150K–$500Kannualized run-rate by end of phase

What Clear Path Builds

  • Governed treasury account UX
  • Role-based admin + approvals
  • Recoverable wallet-backed access
  • Treasury dashboard by fund
  • Premium fund traceability
  • Yield/reporting surfaces

What Partners Provide

  • MPC / embedded wallet infrastructure
  • Stablecoin / treasury / yield integrations
  • Regulated balance housing / custody
  • Fiat ↔ stablecoin conversion / redemption

Phase 3 — Controlled Spend + Card Programs

Turn treasury into deployment

Cards
The story becomes control, deployment, and accountability—not just lower fees.
GoalMake Clear Path a money deployment platform, not just a giving and treasury layer.
Target CustomersChurches with ministry/team budgets, nonprofits with program disbursement needs, and organizations that need constrained spend.
Main Revenue AddsInterchange, premium controls/reporting, and accelerated payout / premium treasury actions.
Revenue Target$500K–$1.5M+annualized run-rate by end of phase

What Clear Path Builds

  • Card program UX for admins and users
  • Budget / policy / role engine
  • Spend approval + exception handling
  • Fund-to-expense reconciliation
  • Advanced traceability and audit workflows

What Partners Provide

  • Card issuing infrastructure
  • Sponsor bank / issuer relationship
  • Authorization stream integrations
  • Partner-led card ops / compliance

Phase 4 — Mission Operating System

Original vision, now with real infrastructure behind it

Mission
Use the proven infrastructure to deliver money to the needy with more dignity, more control, and much stronger donor trust.
GoalApply the full stack to homelessness, housing stability, benevolence, and emergency support programs.
Target CustomersHousing stability nonprofits, re-entry organizations, benevolence programs, and faith-based support initiatives.
Main Revenue AddsInterchange, program reporting/compliance features, treasury/yield economics where appropriate, and program administration revenue.
Revenue Target$1M+annualized run-rate by end of phase

What Clear Path Builds vs Implements

Build

  • Internal ledger / source of truth
  • Fund logic and restrictions
  • Donor and org UX
  • Treasury dashboard
  • Approvals and permissions
  • Restricted-fund traceability
  • Reporting and audit surfaces
  • Policy engine for cards / budgets / permissions

Implement via Partners

  • ACH/card acceptance rails
  • Bank-linking / verification
  • Regulated money movement
  • Custody / housing of funds
  • Yield products
  • Stablecoin treasury infra
  • Card issuing + sponsor-bank stack
  • Authorization stream / network plumbing

Revenue Philosophy

Preferred pricing posture: Clear Path should avoid ugly upfront pricing where possible and get paid when value is created.
  • Transaction economics where unavoidable
  • Yield share when balances produce value
  • Interchange when spend programs are active
  • Premium reporting / controls where they add real value

Raleigh Launch → Expansion

Local launch target

  • 3–10 organizations in Raleigh/Cary/Triangle
  • 1 church with meaningful giving volume
  • 1 nonprofit with restricted-fund use case
  • 1 visible reference customer

Expansion trigger

  • 3 strong proof points
  • Repeatable implementation
  • Stable economics
  • One clear dominant sales story

Suggested expansion order

  • Triangle region
  • Broader North Carolina
  • Selective Southeast expansion

Final View

The right sequence:
1. Save customers money quickly
2. Earn trust with better reporting and fund tracing
3. Turn trust into treasury adoption
4. Turn treasury into controlled deployment and stronger revenue
5. Use that infrastructure to fulfill the original mission
