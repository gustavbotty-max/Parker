# Clear Path — Phased Vision
## Raleigh Launch Plan, Revenue Model, Tech Stack, Partner Stack, and Expansion Path

_Date: 2026-03-23_

---

# 1) Executive Summary

Clear Path should **not** launch as the full final vision on day one.

It should launch as a **high-trust financial operating layer for churches and nonprofits**, then expand into treasury, controlled spend, and eventually the original mission-driven support infrastructure.

## The sequence

### Phase 1 — **Save money and earn trust**
Start with:
- ACH migration
- transparent giving
- restricted-fund traceability
- simple reporting

### Phase 2 — **Turn trust into treasury adoption**
Add:
- governed treasury accounts
- yield participation
- better balance management
- role-based approvals

### Phase 3 — **Turn treasury into controlled spend**
Add:
- org-issued cards
- spend controls
- ministry/program budgets
- stronger interchange economics

### Phase 4 — **Realize the original mission**
Use the proven infrastructure for:
- homelessness / housing stability
- benevolence-to-need loops
- beneficiary controls
- donor-grade transparency

## Operating model
Clear Path should be:
- **the software orchestrator**
- **the rules engine**
- **the ledger + reporting layer**
- **the fund-tracing and treasury UX layer**

Regulated partners should handle:
- money movement
- custody / housing of funds
- yield products
- card issuance
- regulated bank / MTL functions

---

# 2) Core Principles

## Principle 1 — Launch local first
Start in:
- Raleigh
- Cary
- the Triangle

Local trust is a real competitive advantage.

## Principle 2 — Lead with obvious value
The first wins should be measurable and easy to explain.

Best examples:
- lower fee drag
- easier ACH migration
- restricted-fund visibility
- clearer reporting

## Principle 3 — Avoid acting like a bank
Clear Path should live in the **embedded-finance / neo-bank world**, not the “surprise shadow bank” world.

## Principle 4 — Wallet-backed, not wallet-first
If Solana/USDC is real infrastructure, users should still experience:
- governed business accounts
- role-based access
- recoverable login
- approvals

Not:
- raw self-custody wallet chaos

## Principle 5 — Keep the mission, sequence the market
The original vision still matters.
But the first commercial wedge should be easier to sell and easier to adopt.

# Phase 1 — Trust + Savings Wedge
## **Raleigh launch phase**

## What this phase is for
Prove that Clear Path can save organizations money, improve trust, and produce visible ROI quickly.

## Target customers
- mid-sized churches in Raleigh / Cary / Triangle
- smaller nonprofits with recurring donation flows
- organizations frustrated with Realm, Vanco, or other legacy stacks

## Core promise
- keep more of every gift
- reduce card-fee drag through ACH migration
- make restricted funds easier to trace and trust
- don’t charge an ugly software tax upfront

---

## Phase 1 scorecard

### Switch incentive
**Primary switch incentive:** ACH migration

#### Offer ideas
- one-time ACH setup incentive
- one-time fund credit when a recurring donor switches from card to ACH
- church-level ACH migration campaign

#### Why it works
This gives Clear Path a very simple early proof point:

> “We helped you save ~2% and we’re just getting started.”

That is a much better first sales story than asking customers to believe a giant future roadmap.

---

### Revenue target
#### End-of-phase target
- **$25K–$75K annualized run-rate**

#### Per-customer target
- **$3K–$10K annual value per organization**

#### Main revenue sources
- transaction economics / fee share
- ACH migration improving margin
- light premium reporting revenue
- possibly modest early yield-share if partner structure exists

---

### Tech stack
#### Build
- donor-facing giving flow
- organization admin dashboard
- internal ledger / source of truth
- fund designation logic
- restricted-fund reporting
- ACH migration UX
- standard donor reporting
- premium traceability reporting
- basic role-based access

#### Implement via partners
- ACH/card payment rails
- bank account linking / verification
- regulated fund movement
- custody / account housing where needed

---

### Partner stack
- payment processor / ACH-card partner
- bank-linking provider (Plaid-like)
- sponsor bank / regulated movement partner
- optional wallet/MPC provider if wallet-backed treasury already exists lightly in the background

---

### Cost / complexity
#### Complexity level
**Moderate**

#### Main cost buckets
- engineering / contractor time
- processor integration
- hosting / infra
- compliance/legal review
- ACH onboarding / bank-linking costs

#### Most important technical risk
The hardest part is **not** the donation form.
It is the:
- ledger
- fund logic
- trust/reporting model

---

### What this phase adds
#### Technical gain
- foundation ledger
- fund designation + reporting model
- ACH migration mechanics
- trust layer for designated money

#### Business gain
- proves customers will switch
- creates measurable savings case studies
- builds local references

---

### Raleigh launch target
- 3–10 local organizations
- 1 strong church case study
- 1 nonprofit case study
- measurable ACH migration results
- measurable designated-fund transparency value

### Trigger to expand beyond Raleigh
Expand once Clear Path has:
- at least 3 strong local proof points
- repeatable onboarding
- stable unit economics
- strong references / testimonials

### Next geography target
- Triangle region
- then NC metro cluster:
  - Durham

  - Charlotte

> “You already saved on giving. Now your designated balances can work harder while staying easier to manage and report on.”

#### Why this phase matters economically
This is where the “if you win, we win” model begins to feel real.

---

- role-based admin / approval system
- recoverable wallet-backed access model
- treasury dashboard by fund
- premium fund-traceability reporting
- yield/reporting surfaces
- approval workflows for sensitive treasury actions

- wallet / MPC infrastructure
- stablecoin / treasury / yield partner integrations
- regulated balance housing / custody

---

### Partner stack
- MPC / embedded wallet provider
- sponsor bank / account housing partner
- stablecoin / treasury partner

- payout / redemption partner

### Cost / complexity

**High-moderate**

#### Main cost buckets
- wallet / MPC infra

---

### What this phase adds
#### Technical gain
- governed wallet-backed account model
- multi-admin approvals
- treasury visibility
- recoverable access

### Expansion target after Phase 2

# Phase 3 — Controlled Spend + Card Programs

Turn Clear Path into a money deployment platform, not just a giving and treasury layer.

## Target customers
- churches with ministry/team budgets
- nonprofits with program disbursement needs
- housing stability / support organizations
- mission programs needing constraints, auditability, and easier distribution

## Core promise
- issue controlled cards to approved users
- tie spending back to designated funds
- improve oversight without increasing chaos
- make money deployment as traceable as money intake

---

## Phase 3 scorecard

The pitch here becomes:
- better control

- partner terms improve

---

### Tech stack

- sponsor bank / issuer relationships

# 5) Revenue Philosophy

## Preferred pricing posture
Clear Path should avoid ugly upfront pricing where possible.

Best framing:

> **Clear Path gets paid when value is created.**

That means a mix of:
- transaction economics where unavoidable
- yield share when balances produce value
- interchange when spend programs are active
- premium reporting / controls where they add clear value

## Why this is attractive
Churches and nonprofits hate:
- setup fees
- monthly minimums
- software rent before visible ROI

The more Clear Path can say:
- “we win when you win”
- “we saved you money first”
- “premium tools only cost more when they create more value”

…the stronger the commercial story becomes.

The launch should lean into local density and trust.

- measurable ACH migration
- measurable trust/reporting improvement

- repeatable implementation

# 7) Final View

The right sequence is:

1. **save customers money quickly**
2. **earn trust with better reporting and fund tracing**
3. **turn trust into treasury adoption**
4. **turn treasury into controlled deployment and stronger revenue**
5. **use that infrastructure to fulfill the original mission**

That path preserves the soul of Clear Path while giving it a much better chance of actually becoming real.
